Introduction

Learning to control agents directly from high-dimensional sensory inputs like vision and speech is one of the long-standing challenge of RL. And deep learning can be used to resolve this issue. However, RL has several challenges from a DL perspective.

Problems

  1. DL applications require large amounts of hand-labelled training data. RL algorithms, on the other hand, must be able to learn from a scalar reward signal that is delayed. The delay between actions and rewards seems daunting compared to supervised learning.
  2. DL assumes the data samples to be independent, while in RL one encounters sequences of highly correlated states.
  3. In RL the data distribution changes as learning new behaviours, while DL algorithms assume a fixed distribution. This paper demonstrates that a CNN can overcome these challenges to learn successful control policies from raw video data. To alleviate the problems of correlated data(2) and non-stationary distributions(3), we use an experience replay which randomly samples previous transitions, and thereby smooths the training distribution over many past behaviors.

Deep Reinforcement Learning

Experience Replay

Experience replay: store the agent’s experiences at each time-step, et=(st,at,rt,st+1)e_t = (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})et​=(st​,at​,rt​,st+1​) in a dataset D=e1,⋯ ,eN\mathcal{D} = e_1, \cdots, e_ND=e1​,⋯,eN​, into a replay memory. e∼De \sim \mathcal{D}e∼D drawn at random from the pool of stored samples. After performing experience replay, the agent selects and excutes an action according to an ϵ\epsilonϵ-greedy policy. Advantages 1. Each step of experience can be used in many weight updates, allowing greater data efficiency. 2. Randomizing the samples breaks correlations and therefore reduces the variance of updates.

In practice, it stores N experience tuples in the replay memory, and samples uniformly at random from D\mathcal{D}D.
